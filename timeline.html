1758–1806

The life, year by year

Forty-seven years, thirty-four of them in the Navy. The red marks are the days that made his name.

  1. Norfolk, 1758–1770

  2. 175829 Sept.

    Born at Burnham Thorpe

    Sixth of the eleven children of the Reverend Edmund Nelson and Catherine Suckling. Baptised in haste because he seemed unlikely to live.

  3. 176726 Dec.

    His mother dies

    Catherine Nelson dies at forty-two, leaving eight children. Horatio is nine. He is sent to board at Norwich and then at Sir William Paston's School, North Walsham.

  4. Learning the trade, 1771–1779

  5. 17711 Jan.

    Midshipman in the Raisonnable

    Entered on the books of his uncle Maurice Suckling's ship, aged twelve. Later that year he sails to the West Indies before the mast in a merchantman.

  6. 1773June–Sept.

    To the Arctic

    Coxswain to the captain of the bomb vessel Carcass on Phipps's expedition towards the North Pole. The ships are caught in the ice north of Spitsbergen. He chases a polar bear.

  7. 1773to 1776

    The East Indies

    Two years in the frigate Seahorse. Hears his first shots in February 1775. Invalided home with malaria, he resolves in the depths of despair to “be a hero”.

  8. 17779 April

    Lieutenant

    Passes his examination and is appointed next day to the frigate Lowestoffe, bound for Jamaica under William Locker, his lifelong mentor.

  9. 17788 Dec.

    First command

    Commander of the brig Badger, at twenty.

  10. 177911 June

    Post-captain

    Captain of the frigate Hinchinbrook, three months before his twenty-first birthday. From here promotion to admiral comes by seniority alone.

  11. A captain in peace and war, 1780–1792

  12. 1780March–April

    The San Juan river

    Leads the boats of an expedition against a Spanish fort in the Nicaraguan jungle. Fever destroys the force and all but kills him.

  13. 1781to 1783

    Albemarle

    The Baltic, Quebec, New York and the West Indies. Meets Prince William Henry and Lord Hood. Fails to retake Turks Island from the French.

  14. 1784to 1787

    Boreas, Leeward Islands

    Enforces the Navigation Acts against American traders and makes himself the most unpopular man in the islands.

  15. 178711 March

    Marries Frances Nisbet

    At Montpelier on the island of Nevis. The future William IV gives the bride away.

  16. 1788to 1793

    On the beach

    Five years on half-pay at his father's parsonage, farming, reading the papers and begging the Admiralty for a ship.

  17. The war with France, 1793–1797

  18. 1793Jan.

    Agamemnon

    France declares war on 1 February; Nelson already has a 64-gun ship. In September he is sent to Naples for troops and meets Sir William and Lady Hamilton.

  19. 179412 July

    Calvi

    Commanding naval batteries ashore in Corsica, he is struck in the face by stones thrown up by a shot and loses the sight of his right eye.

  20. 179513 March

    The Ça Ira

    Agamemnon rakes a crippled French 80 for two and a half hours. The admiral declines to follow up. Nelson is disgusted.

  21. 1796April

    Commodore

    Sir John Jervis takes command in the Mediterranean and recognises him at once. Nelson moves to the 74-gun Captain. That winter the Navy abandons the Mediterranean.

  22. 179714 Feb.

    Cape St Vincent

    Leaves the line without orders, heads off the Spanish van, and boards San Nicolás and San Josef. Promoted rear-admiral; made a Knight of the Bath.

  23. 179724 July

    Tenerife

    A night assault on Santa Cruz fails with heavy loss. His right arm is shattered and amputated. He goes home expecting never to be employed again.

  24. The Nile and Naples, 1798–1800

  25. 179829 March

    Vanguard

    Hoists his flag and is sent into the Mediterranean to find Bonaparte's expedition. A gale dismasts his flagship and scatters his frigates.

  26. 17981 Aug.

    The Nile

    Destroys the French fleet in Aboukir Bay. Wounded in the head. Created Baron Nelson of the Nile.

  27. 179822 Sept.

    Naples

    A hero's welcome and Emma Hamilton's nursing. In December, with a French army advancing, he carries the royal family to Palermo.

  28. 1799June

    The retaking of Naples

    Sets aside the surrender terms granted to the Neapolitan republicans and has Admiral Caracciolo hanged. Created Duke of Bronté by a grateful king. See legacy.

  29. 1800July–Nov.

    Home across Europe

    Recalled, he travels overland with the Hamiltons through Vienna, Prague, Dresden and Hamburg, and lands at Great Yarmouth on 6 November.

  30. Copenhagen and the peace, 1801–1803

  31. 1801Jan.

    Leaves Fanny; Horatia born

    Promoted vice-admiral on New Year's Day. Parts from his wife for good within weeks. Emma gives birth to his daughter at the end of the month.

  32. 18012 April

    Copenhagen

    Attacks the Danish line, disregards the signal of recall, and negotiates an armistice. Created a viscount; given the Baltic command.

  33. 180115 Aug.

    Boulogne

    A boat attack on the French invasion flotilla is beaten off. In September he buys Merton Place; in October, with peace coming, he strikes his flag.

  34. 180226 April

    His father dies

    Nelson does not go to the funeral. That summer he tours Wales and the Midlands with the Hamiltons, cheered in every town.

  35. 18036 April

    Sir William Hamilton dies

    In Emma's arms, with Nelson holding his hand.

  36. The last command, 1803–1805

  37. 180318 May

    Commander-in-chief, Mediterranean

    War begins again. He hoists his flag in Victory at Portsmouth and joins the fleet off Toulon in July. He will not set foot ashore for two years.

  38. 180423 April

    Vice-Admiral of the White

    His last promotion.

  39. 1805May–July

    The long chase

    Villeneuve escapes from Toulon and makes for the West Indies. Nelson follows with ten ships, drives him back to Europe, and returns having sailed seven thousand miles.

  40. 180520 Aug.–13 Sept.

    Twenty-five days at Merton

    His last leave. He meets Wellington by chance, explains his plan of attack to friends on the garden path, and on the night of the 13th prays at his daughter's bedside and leaves.

  41. 180528 Sept.

    Off Cádiz

    Takes over the fleet on the eve of his birthday. Over two dinners he explains “the Nelson touch” to his captains, and on 9 October issues it as a memorandum.

  42. 180521 Oct.

    Trafalgar

    Shot at 1.15 pm on Victory's quarterdeck. Dies at 4.30 pm with eighteen enemy ships taken or destroyed.

  43. Afterwards

  44. 18056 Nov.

    The news reaches London

    Lieutenant Lapenotière of the schooner Pickle arrives at the Admiralty at one in the morning after a 37-hour ride from Falmouth.

  45. 18069 Jan.

    State funeral

    After lying in state at Greenwich and a procession of barges up the Thames, he is buried in the crypt of St Paul's Cathedral.

  46. 181515 Jan.

    Emma dies

    In lodgings in Calais, in debt.

  47. 18314 May

    Fanny dies

    Frances, Viscountess Nelson, in London, twenty-six years a widow.

  48. 1843Nov.

    The column

    E. H. Baily's statue is raised to the top of Nelson's Column in Trafalgar Square.

  49. 18816 March

    Horatia dies

    His daughter, Mrs Horatia Nelson Ward, at eighty.

“Thank God, I have done my duty.”
